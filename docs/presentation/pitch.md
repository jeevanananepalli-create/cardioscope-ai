# CardioScope AI — pitch

**From clinical numbers to anatomical insight.**

*Research and education prototype. Not a medical device.*

## One sentence

CardioScope AI turns routine clinical measurements into four explained model predictions — coronary
artery disease and stenosis of the LAD, LCX and RCA — and shows them on an interactive 3D heart,
while being explicit about what the models can and cannot support.

## The problem

Risk models usually return a number. A number is hard to interpret, easy to over-trust, and says
nothing about how good the model behind it is.

## What is different here

| Usual prototype | CardioScope AI |
|---|---|
| One risk score | Four predictions, one per outcome, each with its own model |
| Headline accuracy | Holdout metrics with intervals, next to a no-information reference |
| "The model found…" | "Model prediction", with the evidence for that model beside it |
| Feature importance as fact | SHAP contributions, worded as how the model used its inputs |
| A decorative 3D heart | Real reference anatomy where only the three modelled vessels carry model colour |
| Leakage avoided by care | Leakage blocked in code and proven by tests |

## What a viewer sees in two minutes

1. A safety disclaimer that never leaves the screen.
2. One click on a demo profile: four probabilities, and the coronary arteries change colour.
3. A click on the LAD: its probability, the inputs that drove it, and that model's track record.
4. What-if: change one input and watch all four outputs and the heart respond, labelled exploratory.
5. Model performance: the real numbers, including the two models that are weak.

The CAD and LAD models clearly beat the no-information reference. The LCX and RCA models are weak —
the RCA model is barely above it — and the application shows that wherever their numbers appear.

## Why the honesty is the feature

A prototype that shows a 3D artery turning red invites the viewer to believe something was found.
Everything in CardioScope AI is built to prevent that reading: neutral colours for everything the
models do not predict, model evidence beside every prediction, non-causal wording, a labelled
simulation mode, and results that include the failures.

## What it would take to go further

External validation, clinical leadership, more and broader data, and regulatory assessment. None of
that has been done, and the project does not claim otherwise.
